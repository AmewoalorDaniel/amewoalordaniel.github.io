## Education & Research Interests

### Background

My scientific interests stem from a fascination with understanding the biological mechanisms that drive human disease and the development of therapeutic strategies to combat them. During my undergraduate studies in biomedical science, I became increasingly interested in how the immune system, genomics, and computational biology can be leveraged to better understand disease processes and improve therapeutic outcomes, particularly in cancer.

My early research experiences exposed me to both experimental molecular biology and computational approaches in biomedical research. These experiences strengthened my interest in translational science and motivated me to pursue interdisciplinary research integrating wet lab experimentation with computational analysis.

Throughout my academic training, I have developed a strong interest in cancer immunology, immunogenomics, tumor heterogeneity, and computational approaches for studying disease biology. I am particularly interested in understanding how molecular and immune interactions shape disease progression and therapeutic responses.

---

### Undergraduate Research & Scientific Development

My undergraduate research experiences provided me with exposure to both laboratory and computational biomedical research. During an internship at Borquaye Research, I investigated the antimicrobial activities of medicinal plant extracts and studied molecular interactions between bioactive compounds and microbial targets using cheminformatics approaches. This experience introduced me to scientific research, strengthened my laboratory skills, and sparked my broader interest in disease biology and therapeutic development.

Subsequently, I gained experience in molecular genetics and biomedical laboratory techniques through research training involving DNA extraction, quantification, and molecular analysis. These experiences strengthened my understanding of experimental biology and exposed me to the importance of rigorous biomedical research.

Alongside my experimental training, I developed a growing interest in computational biology and immunology. My undergraduate research project focused on the in silico development of a peptide vaccine against lymphatic filariasis using immunoinformatics approaches. Motivated by the growing success of immune-based therapies and vaccines in combating disease, I designed and evaluated peptide vaccine constructs predicted to induce antigenic and immunogenic CD8+ T-cell responses. Through this work, I gained experience in computational vaccine design, epitope prediction, immune simulation, and bioinformatics-driven therapeutic development.

My research interests further expanded into cancer biology following participation in oncology-focused educational activities and independent exploration of cancer immunology and immunotherapy. I became fascinated by how immune responses, tumor heterogeneity, and genomic alterations influence cancer progression and therapeutic outcomes. This growing interest motivated me to pursue additional training in genomics, transcriptomics, and computational biology.

---

### Current Research Interests

My current interests lie at the intersection of cancer immunology, genomics, computational biology, and translational biomedical research. I am particularly interested in integrating experimental and computational approaches to investigate tumor-immune interactions, molecular drivers of disease, and mechanisms underlying therapeutic responses.

I have experience analyzing high-throughput sequencing data, including RNA-seq differential gene expression analysis using Bioconductor packages such as DESeq2 and edgeR. I am also interested in cancer predisposition genomics, variant prioritization, tumor microenvironment biology, and immunogenomics.

In parallel with computational approaches, I remain deeply interested in experimental biomedical research and hope to continue developing expertise in molecular and cellular immunology techniques alongside computational genomics and bioinformatics.

Ultimately, my long-term goal is to contribute to translational cancer research by integrating wet lab experimentation and computational analysis to better understand disease biology and support the development of improved therapeutic strategies.
