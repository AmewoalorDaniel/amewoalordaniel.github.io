---

## Background

My scientific journey began during my undergraduate training in Biomedical Science, where I developed a dual interest in experimental molecular biology and computational approaches to understanding human disease.

During an internship at **Borquaye Research**, I investigated the antimicrobial activities of medicinal plant extracts and explored molecular interactions between bioactive compounds and microbial targets using cheminformatics. This experience sharpened my laboratory skills and deepened my interest in disease biology and therapeutic development.

My undergraduate research project centered on the **in silico design of a peptide vaccine against lymphatic filariasis** using immunoinformatics. I designed and evaluated peptide vaccine constructs predicted to elicit CD8⁺ T-cell responses, gaining hands-on experience in epitope prediction, immune simulation, and computational vaccine development.

---

## Current Work

My current work sits at the intersection of **cancer genomics, immunology, and bioinformatics**. I have experience analyzing high-throughput sequencing data, including RNA-seq differential expression analysis using DESeq2 and edgeR (Bioconductor). Recent projects include differential gene expression analysis in hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) and computational investigation of HCC vaccine targets.

I am interested in understanding how tumor-immune interactions, molecular heterogeneity, and genomic alterations shape cancer progression and therapeutic outcomes — and in translating these insights toward improved therapeutic strategies.

---

## Long-Term Goals

I aim to contribute to translational cancer research by combining rigorous wet lab experimentation with computational genomics and bioinformatics — bridging the gap between molecular biology and data-driven discovery to better understand disease and support therapeutic development.
