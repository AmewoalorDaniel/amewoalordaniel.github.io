My research examines how genomic alterations and immune dysregulation drive cancer and other complex diseases at the intersection of cancer biology, immunology, human genetics, and computational biology, with a focus on clinically meaningful discoveries for underrepresented populations and the world.
---

#### Cancer Immunology and Immunotherapy

<!-- Upload a figure to /images/ and uncomment:
<img src="/images/cancer-immunity.png" alt="Cancer immunology"
     style="float:right; width:300px; margin:0 0 15px 25px; border-radius:6px;"> -->

I am interested in understanding how the immune system can be harnessed and reprogrammed to recognise and eliminate tumour cells. In particular, I am fascinated by mechanisms of immune evasion, neoantigen expression, and the development of next-generation immunotherapies, including peptide and mRNA vaccines, immune checkpoint inhibitors, and adoptive cell-based therapies.

A major focus of my research is improving tumour specificity and predicting patient response to immunotherapy through molecular characterisation of the tumour immune microenvironment. I am fascinated by how regulated cell death pathways, especially ferroptosis and oxeiptosis, influence tumour-associated immune cell states and whether these pathways can be therapeutically targeted to enhance anti-tumour immunity.

#### Cancer Genomics and Tumour Microenvironment Biology

<!-- <img src="/images/tumour-microenvironment.png" alt="Tumour microenvironment"
     style="float:right; width:300px; margin:0 0 15px 25px; border-radius:6px;"> -->

I seek to understand how somatic mutations, copy-number alterations, and disruptions in driver genes reshape the tumour microenvironment and influence immune behaviour in cancers. I am particularly interested in how genomic and immunological features interact to define tumour progression, therapeutic response, and potential vulnerabilities that can be exploited therapeutically.

My recent work has focused on tumour-associated macrophages and their functional heterogeneity across cancers. I am interested in how distinct macrophage transcriptional states emerge, how they contribute to tumour progression or immune suppression, and how ferroptosis-related regulatory programs shape these cellular phenotypes. More broadly, I aim to investigate how genomic alterations influence immune landscapes and how this knowledge can guide neoantigen discovery, patient stratification, and precision immunotherapy design.

#### Human Genetics of Rare and Complex Disease

<!-- <img src="/images/rare-disease-genetics.png" alt="Rare disease genomics"
     style="float:right; width:300px; margin:0 0 15px 25px; border-radius:6px;"> -->

Another major area of my research interest is the application of whole-exome and whole-genome sequencing to understand the genetic basis of rare and complex diseases in African populations. Despite carrying immense genetic diversity and a significant disease burden, African populations remain substantially underrepresented in global genomics studies, a gap that continues to limit both biological understanding and clinical translation.

I focus on how rare germline variants contribute to distinct disease phenotypes, especially the overlap between developmental disorders and cancer susceptibility. My ongoing project explores these intersections through the study of syndromic orofacial clefts and hereditary cancer predisposition. More broadly, I am motivated by research that identifies clinically actionable variants, improves genetic diagnosis, and connects molecular findings to disease mechanisms through structural and functional analysis.

#### Computational Biology and Multi-Omics Integration

<!-- <img src="/images/computational-biology.png" alt="Computational biology"
     style="float:right; width:300px; margin:0 0 15px 25px; border-radius:6px;"> -->

I use computational biology and multi-omics integration to understand disease at a systems level. By integrating transcriptomic, genomic, and single cell sequencing data, I examine how molecular heterogeneity shapes disease progression, immune behaviour, and treatment outcomes.

---

#### Translational and Global Health Research

Underlying all of my research interests is a strong commitment to translational science and global health relevance. I am motivated by the challenge of moving genomic and immunological discoveries toward clinically actionable insights, particularly in resource-limited settings and for diseases that disproportionately affect African populations.

Rigorous basic science and real world clinical impact are not separate goals; they are deeply connected.  I aim to produce research that is scientifically robust, globally relevant, and capable of improving healthcare outcomes in underrepresented communities.
